Robots as Learning Companions at Home

Social robots can make learning feel like a shared, playful activity, and large language models now let them hold open-ended conversations. This line of work explores how robots can become learning companions for young children in their everyday lives, including at home.

Our first study designed an LLM-powered humanoid social robot that reads AI literacy books with children ages 5–8, the same kind of books we analyze in Children's Books About AI, and talks with them about the story and the AI ideas in it.

Highlights

  • Integrates speech, vision, language generation, and gesture to support multimodal interaction with children.
  • Implements and compares three interaction policies (heuristic, dynamic, and guided) that give different levels of control over LLM-mediated dialogue.
  • Runs on real robot hardware and was tested through controlled interaction evaluation.

Findings

  • Deploying the reading companion on real robot hardware is feasible.
  • A structured interaction framework with bounded LLM support produced more robust and pedagogically coherent interactions than either fully scripted or fully open-ended dialogue.
  • The results point to design directions for safe and effective embodied AI learning companions for young children.
