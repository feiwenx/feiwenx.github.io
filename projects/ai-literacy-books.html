Children's Books About AI

Picture books are one of the first places many young children meet the idea of artificial intelligence. A growing number of books now set out to teach AI to young readers, but their quality and educational value had not been examined systematically.

This project asks what these books actually teach. Using the AI4K12 “Five Big Ideas in AI” framework (perception, representation and reasoning, learning, natural interaction, and societal impact), we study how AI literacy books present foundational AI ideas, practices, and values to children.

Highlights

  • Built a corpus of 57 English-language AI literacy books written for children ages 5–8.
  • Conducted a mixed-methods content analysis, combining qualitative coding with per-book frequency and cluster analyses.
  • Received the Best Paper Award at AIED 2026; a companion paper was presented at ICLS 2026.
  • Brought the findings into a graduate course module at Penn, where students designed and critiqued children's AI literacy books.

Findings

  • The books concentrate on three Big Ideas: Societal Impact, Learning, and Natural Interaction.
  • Perception and Representation & Reasoning, the ideas that explain how AI systems work under the hood, appear much less often.
  • A cluster analysis identified five recurring book profiles, each built around a different combination of Big Ideas.
  • Overall, the books favor socially meaningful stories over explanations of how AI works, pointing to a need for age-appropriate resources that also open up AI's inner workings.
